Phrases while you speak

You asked for a filter-rail-style rail with useful Redemittel in the practice conversations. Right now the app names the four Redemittel categories before the conversation and ticks them off after it, but never shows a single actual phrase while the learner is talking, which is the only moment they need one. Below: today's screens, then three places the phrases could live. Two switches at the top change what the rail holds in every option, so you can answer layout and content in one reply.

What a learner sees today

The brief card lists the four Redemittel categories as grey labels. Then the conversation starts, the brief collapses to one line, and the phrases are gone. The bank has eight phrases behind each of those four labels, in the Bibliothek, in another zone.

1
Category names are not language

“Vorschläge machen” tells a B1 learner what to do, not how to say it. The eight phrases that would let them do it are one page away and two taps out of reach mid-conversation.

2
The one moment they matter is the one moment they are missing

The words are on screen before the conversation and after it, never during. The debrief then grades whether those Redemittel were used.

3
Nothing on the running screen is a rail

The conversation is a single 672 px column: collapsed brief, transcript, microphone. There is empty space beside it on a desktop and none at all on a phone.

True in all three options

A
Practice only, never the Modelltest

The exam grades whether the candidate reached for these phrases. Handing them the list during Teil Sprechen would grade the reading, not the speaking. Say the word if you want it in the exam too.

B
The phrases are the task's own

Each scenario already names four Redemittel categories, and the debrief already grades against exactly those. No new content is authored: the rail reads the existing bank.

C
Reading a phrase is never a tick

Whether a Redemittel was used stays a question for the model, judged from what was actually said. Tapping one in the rail proves nothing and marks nothing.

D
The tile is the rail you already approved

Same Himmelblau fill, same uppercase section labels, same dropdown, same reset icon as “Aufgabe wählen”. Nothing new is invented.

Option A

A rail beside the conversation

The literal answer to your prompt. On a desktop the conversation keeps its column and a 256 px Redemittel rail appears beside it, in the same Himmelblau tile as “Aufgabe wählen”: a dropdown that picks the speech intent, the phrases for it as white cards, and the task's other three intents as pills below. On a phone the same tile opens as a panel over the transcript, behind one toggle next to the collapsed brief.

Prüfung

Most phrases visible at once. Three to eight, with room for the English line, and it can be browsed while the partner is talking.

It is the rail language, so it reads as part of the app rather than as a feature bolted onto the speaking screen.

Desktop gets it for free: the space beside the conversation is currently empty.

Cost: on a phone it covers the transcript while open, so the learner reads either their conversation or their phrases, not both. The stage cannot grow, and the rule that a running screen never scrolls the page holds.

Option B

A phrase strip above the microphone

One row of phrase chips directly above the mic, with the four intents as small pills over it. Tapping a chip opens it to the full phrase (and its English). Identical on desktop and phone, and it sits where the learner's eyes already are in the second before they speak.

Noch 9 Beiträge

Prüfung

No new element on the screen. The disclosure the learner already uses simply holds one more thing.

Desktop and phone are the same layout, which keeps one thing to build and one thing to maintain.

Two columns of phrases on a desktop, one on a phone.

Cost: open, it eats the top third of the transcript, and the goals and the phrases are mutually exclusive. It also does not look like a rail, which is what you asked for.

The two content switches

1
Three phrases per intent, or all eight

Eight is everything the bank holds, and on a phone it is a list to read instead of a conversation to have. Three is the strongest three, with the rest one tap away in the dropdown. The switch at the top of this page shows both.

2
du / Sie

Sabine Berger says Sie, so “Was hältst du davon, wenn …?” is the wrong phrase in this conversation. Filtering it out is the one thing here that genuinely earns the word “filter”. The catch: the bank tags phrases neutral / formal, not du / Sie, so this needs a small derived rule reading the phrase text. It goes in one place, like the gap rule in the writing clozes, never copied per call site.

3
The English line

Hold-to-peek is the app's pattern everywhere else, and it keeps the rail short. Always visible doubles the height of every row but never needs a press mid-conversation.

How to reply

Name the option and the two switches, for example “A, 3 per intent, match the partner, hold to peek”. If you want the rail in the Modelltest as well, or a different name than “Redemittel” on the tile, say it in the same reply and it goes into this round.
